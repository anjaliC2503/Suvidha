## Brief Description

The objective of the scheme is to promote the cultivation of ginger in the North Eastern region by supporting farmers with financial assistance for quality planting material, enhancing productivity and quality, and increasing the exportable surplus of spices from the region.

## Detailed Description

The ‘Promotion of Exportable Surplus of Identified Spices in the North East: Cultivation of Ginger’, implemented by the Spices Board, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Government of India, financial assistance is provided at 50% of the cost of planting material, subject to a maximum of ₹30,000 per hectare. The scheme is applicable to ginger growers in the North Eastern region with land holdings ranging from 0.10 ha to 8 ha, including Sikkim, Darjeeling and Kalimpong districts of West Bengal, and other North Eastern states. Designated officials will conduct final inspections and recommend eligible cases.
<br>

## Benefits

- 50% of cost of planting material subject to maximum of ₹ 30,000/- per Ha
- Ginger growers of NE having land holding size of 0.10 Ha to 8 Ha

<br>

## Eligibility

1. The applicant must be a ginger grower from Sikkim, Darjeeling and Kalimpong districts of West Bengal, or other North Eastern states.
1. Eligibility is subject to final inspection and recommendation by designated officials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Promotion of Exportable Surplus of Identified Spices in the North East: Cultivation of Ginger scheme?

The scheme is a financial assistance programme implemented by the Spices Board under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry to encourage ginger cultivation in the North Eastern region of India. It supports farmers by providing subsidy assistance for quality planting material so that production, quality, and export potential of ginger can improve across the region.
<br>

### What financial assistance is available under the scheme?

Under the scheme, financial assistance is provided at 50 percent of the cost of planting material used for ginger cultivation. However, the maximum assistance allowed is ₹30,000 per hectare. The remaining expenditure must be borne by the farmer.
<br>

### What does the term “planting material” mean in this scheme?

Planting material refers to quality ginger seed rhizomes or certified propagating materials used for cultivation. The scheme encourages farmers to use healthy, disease-free, and high-quality planting material to ensure better yield and improved marketability.
<br>

### Is there any minimum or maximum land requirement for eligibility?

Yes, farmers must cultivate ginger on at least 0.10 hectare of land to qualify under the scheme. The maximum eligible land holding covered under the programme is 8 hectares.
<br>

### Can small and marginal farmers also benefit from this scheme?

Yes, small and marginal farmers are one of the major target groups of this programme. As long as they meet the minimum land requirement and cultivate ginger in eligible regions, they can apply for financial assistance.
<br>

### How is the subsidy amount calculated for farmers?

The subsidy is calculated based on the actual cost incurred for purchasing planting material. Since the assistance covers only 50 percent of the cost, the farmer receives reimbursement up to the prescribed ceiling of ₹30,000 per hectare.
<br>

### Which authority is responsible for implementing the scheme?

The scheme is implemented and supervised by the Spices Board through its regional and field offices operating in the North Eastern region.
<br>

### Are inspections conducted before approving the financial assistance?

Yes, designated officials conduct field inspections before approving assistance. During inspection, officials verify the cultivated area, use of planting material, and compliance with scheme conditions. Only eligible and verified cases are recommended for subsidy release.
<br>

### Can tenant farmers or lease cultivators apply for the scheme?

Tenant farmers or lease cultivators may also apply if they can provide valid proof of cultivation or lease agreements. Final approval depends on verification by the concerned Spices Board authorities.
<br>

### What are the major objectives of the ginger cultivation scheme?

The scheme aims to increase ginger production in the North Eastern region, improve the quality of exportable ginger, promote scientific cultivation practices, strengthen spice exports, and improve the income and livelihood of farmers engaged in spice cultivation.
<br>

### Where can farmers obtain application forms and further information?

Farmers can approach the nearest office of the Spices Board for application forms, eligibility details, and guidance related to the scheme. Additional information is also available on the official website.
<br>
